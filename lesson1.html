Lesson 1: Understanding Data

What is Data?

IBM describes data as collections of facts, numbers, words, observations, or other pieces of information that can be used for reference or analysis.

In its raw form, data is simply a set of symbols, values, or recorded details that represent real measurements or abstract ideas. A single piece of data (called a datum) doesn’t tell us much on its own. Data becomes meaningful only after it is organised, processed, and analysed to reveal patterns, trends, or context.

Types of Data

Image Source: Syracuse University School of Information Studies

  • Quantitative Data — Numerical information that can be measured or counted, such as height, temperature, or money in a bank account.
  • Qualitative Data — Descriptive information based on characteristics or qualities, such as colours, spoken words, audio, or video.

Sub‑Types of Data

  • Discrete Data — Countable whole numbers that cannot be easily divided into fractions. Examples include the number of students in a classroom or the number of cars in a car park.
  • Continuous Data — Measurements that can take any value within a range. Examples include height, weight, and temperature.
  • Nominal Data — Categories or labels with no specific order. Examples include hair colour, gender, or blood type.
  • Ordinal Data — Categories that follow a clear ranking or order. Examples include survey responses (good, average, poor) or education levels.
